# Colors

Every color in the Web App is a semantic token. A token is a CSS variable that
`@exponetdb/tokens` derives, in OKLCH, from a handful of inputs for a light and a
dark theme. Components and pages name a role (`bg-card`, `text-foreground-light`,
`border-control`), never a value. Because the roles are derived, they stay
readable together: the contrast between a token and the token it is drawn on is
proven for the whole supported range of the inputs.

## The rules

- **Use roles, not values.** No hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()` or `oklch()` literal, no
  Tailwind palette class (`bg-slate-900`, `text-red-500`) and no color in a
  `style` prop. The palette classes still compile, but they are not part of the
  system and do not follow the theme.
- **A fill always travels with its foreground.** `bg-primary` goes with
  `text-primary-foreground`, `bg-destructive` with `text-destructive-foreground`.
  The foreground flips between ink and paper so the pair holds 4.5:1.
- **Color is never the only signal.** An error also has an icon and words; a
  state also has a shape and a label. Colors carry emphasis, not meaning.
- **Production is reserved.** The `production` token marks the production tier and
  nothing else. See [Production and state colors](/tokens/production-and-state).
- **Change the inputs, not the tokens.** A brand is swapped through the theme
  inputs on `:root`; see [Theme](/tokens/theme).

## Surfaces

A higher surface is a little lighter than the one below it, in both themes. The
step is `--elevation-step` (`0.015` in the light theme, `0.025` in the dark one):
`bg-card` sits one step above the page, `bg-popover` two and `bg-secondary` three.
Put content on the surface that matches its role and let the text tier carry the
hierarchy.

- `bg-background` is the page. `bg-card` (also `bg-surface-100`) is a card or a
  panel that sits on it.
- `bg-muted` (`bg-surface-200`) is a quiet fill and the hover of a row.
  `bg-secondary` (`bg-surface-300`) is a raised plate. `bg-tertiary`
  (`bg-surface-400`) is the strongest neutral fill.
- `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` and `bg-tertiary` are translucent overlays of the text
  color, so they composite with whatever surface is below them. Do not place one
  on an element that has to hide what is behind it; use `bg-card` or
  `bg-popover` for that, or `FloatingPlate` for a default button over busy content.
- `bg-popover` is a popover or a sheet. `bg-overlay` is a menu, a select list or a
  hover card. `bg-accent` (`bg-selection`) is the neutral hover and selection
  surface; the spot color is `primary`, not `accent`.

## Text

Four tiers of text color, all body-text safe: at least 4.5:1 on the page and on
every surface above. There is no "large text only" token.

- `text-foreground` is body text and headings.
- `text-foreground-light` (also `text-muted-foreground`) is secondary text: a
  description, a label, a value that supports another.
- `text-foreground-lighter` (also `text-foreground-muted`) is the quietest tier:
  hints, placeholders, timestamps.
- `text-primary` is a link or a status in the spot color, readable as ink.
  `text-destructive` is an error message. Use accent text sparingly.
- `bg-foreground text-background` is an inverted fill, as on the secondary button.

## Borders

There are two kinds of border, and they are not interchangeable.

- **Hairlines** (`border-default`, `border-muted`, `border-overlay`,
  `border-strong`, `border-stronger`) separate regions. They scale with the
  contrast setting and have no contrast requirement, so they never mark a control
  on their own.
- **Control boundaries** (`border-control`, `border-control-hover`, alias
  `border-input`) outline an input, a select, a checkbox or a radio. They hold
  3:1 on the page and on every surface at every contrast setting (WCAG 1.4.11).

## Fills

Solid fills for actions and status, each with a `-foreground` token.

- `primary` is the spot color. `bg-primary` is for small indicators and ink,
  `bg-primary-solid` is the plate of the primary button (deeper than `primary`
  in the dark theme), and `bg-primary-bright` is for switch fills, selected
  chrome and the focus ring; it holds 3:1 on the page.
- `success`, `warning`, `destructive` and `info` carry status. The hue of each is
  fixed, so an error stays red whatever the product color is.
- Hover moves the lightness only, toward the text color: `primary-hover`,
  `primary-solid-hover`, `success-hover`, `warning-hover`, `destructive-hover`.
- `primary` and the status fills are also readable as text on the page and on a
  card (4.5:1), which is why `text-destructive` works for an error message.

## Stepped scales

For tinted surfaces such as an alert or an input in error, use a scale:
`primary-`, `success-`, `warning-` and `destructive-` from `200` to `600`.

- `200` and `300` are backgrounds. `400` and `500` are borders.
- `600` is text on a `200` or `300` background (at least 4.5:1) and a strong fill.

## Chart slots

Charts read `chart-1` to `chart-8` (`bg-chart-1`, `var(--chart-1)`), a `-fill`
variant of each at 25 percent for areas, and `chart-reference`, `chart-muted` and
`chart-status-*` for thresholds and states. Slot 1 is the spot color; the others
spread by the golden angle and alternate in lightness so neighbors differ. Every
slot holds 3:1 on the page. No hex value ever reaches a chart; see
[Chart](/ui/chart).

## Accessibility

- Text tiers hold 4.5:1; control boundaries, the focus ring and chart slots hold
  3:1. A real-browser sweep checks 114 pairs over both themes, six hues, both
  extremes of every input and three contrast settings.
- The contrast setting never lowers secondary text below its floor. Below the
  baseline only decorative tokens (hairlines, hover overlays) get fainter.
- Never rely on a hairline to show that something can be operated, and never put
  `text-foreground-muted` on a colored fill.

## The inputs

Seven inputs derive everything: `--hue`, `--surface-hue`, `--chroma`, `--surface`,
`--foreground-lightness`, `--contrast` and `--elevation-step`. Their ranges and
the theme mechanism are on the [Theme](/tokens/theme) page. The spot color is a
neutral placeholder, with a turquoise accent chosen by the owner (S9), until the owner supplies a brand (OD-11).
