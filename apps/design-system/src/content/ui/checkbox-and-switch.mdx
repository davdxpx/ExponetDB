# Checkbox and switch

`Checkbox` and `Switch` both hold a boolean, and the difference between them is when
the change takes effect. A checkbox is an **option you choose and then submit**. A
switch is a **setting that is on or off right now**. Pick by that question, not by
how the control looks.

## When to use

- **Checkbox:** an option in a form or a dialog ("Verify after apply"), a choice from a
  list where several may be chosen ("Schemas to compare"), and a confirmation
  that must be ticked ("I have read the lock analysis"). Nothing happens until the
  form is submitted.
- **Switch:** a preference or a policy that is saved the moment it changes
  ("Notify on failure"). A switch whose change needs a confirmation or a plan is the
  wrong control; use a form with a button.
- Do not use either for a choice between two named alternatives ("Staging" or
  "Production"): that is a [Radio group](/ui/radio-group) or a
  [Toggle group](/ui/toggle-group). Do not use a switch inside a form that has a save
  button.
- A destructive or production-affecting setting is never a bare switch. It goes
  through a plan or a confirmation like any other change.

## Anatomy

- **Checkbox:** a 16 px box (`rounded-sm`, `border-control`) with a check mark, or a
  dash when it is indeterminate. A visible label sits next to it and is tied to it
  with `htmlFor`.
- **Switch:** a track and a thumb, in three sizes (**small** 16 px, **medium** 20 px,
  **large** 24 px high). On is the bright spot color. A label sits next to it.
- Both are Radix primitives and forward every Radix prop (`checked`, `defaultChecked`,
  `onCheckedChange`, `disabled`, `name`, `value`, `required`).

## States

- **Unchecked and checked.** A checkbox can also be **indeterminate**
  (`checked="indeterminate"`): some of a group are chosen. Use it for a "select all"
  parent. Pressing it chooses all.
- **Disabled** lowers the opacity and leaves the tab order. If a person needs to know
  why, say so in the label or the description ("set by policy").
- **Focus** draws the focus ring; hover strengthens the border.

## Accessibility

- A checkbox is a `checkbox` and a switch is a `switch`; both toggle with Space. Give
  each a visible label tied with `htmlFor` and `id`, or wrap the pair in
  [`Field`](/ui/field).
- A group of related checkboxes is a `fieldset` with a `legend` or a `role="group"`
  labelled by a heading, so the group has a name.
- The state is the control's `aria-checked`, not its color. An indeterminate
  checkbox reports `mixed`.
- A switch that saves immediately should confirm that it did, with the control itself
  and, where useful, a status line (`role="status"`), and should tell the person when
  saving failed. Do not leave a switch in the new position after a failed save.
- The target is the box plus the label: make the label clickable.

## Copy

- Labels are short phrases that read as true or false: "Verify after apply", "Notify
  on failure". Name the option, not the action ("Verify" alone could be a button).
- Write the positive form. "Notify on failure" is on or off; "Do not notify" invites
  double negatives.
- Do not write "Enable" or "Allow" in front of every label; the control already says
  it is on or off.
