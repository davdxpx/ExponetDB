# Tooltip and hover card

Both open when a control is hovered or focused and close on Escape, and neither takes
focus. A **tooltip** is one short line of text that describes its trigger. A **hover
card** is a small panel with a few lines of supplementary detail. Because both depend
on hover or focus, **nothing essential may live only in them**: the same information
must be reachable some other way.

The parts are `TooltipProvider`, `Tooltip`, `TooltipTrigger`, `TooltipContent`,
`TooltipPortal` and `HoverCard`, `HoverCardTrigger`, `HoverCardContent`.
`TooltipProvider` wraps the app once.

## When to use

- Use a **tooltip** to name an icon-only button, to give the exact value behind an
  abbreviation ("12 min ago" and its timestamp), or to explain why a control is
  unavailable.
- Use a **hover card** for supplementary detail about a named thing: a node's agent
  version and heartbeat next to its name, the source of a state.
- Do not use either for instructions, errors, or information a person needs to act.
  Write that in the page. Do not put links, buttons or form controls in them: they cannot
  be reached.
- On touch screens there is no hover. Radix ignores touch for hover cards and does not
  open a tooltip on a tap. Everything in them is a convenience.

## Anatomy

- **Provider:** `TooltipProvider` once near the root (the app shell provides it).
  Components that contain tooltips, such as the sidebar, assume it.
- **Tooltip content:** a small bordered label, one line, up to about 80 characters, in the
  small text size. It opens above the trigger by default (`side`), with 4 px of offset.
- **Hover card content:** a panel 16 rem wide with the overlay surface, padded, for a few
  lines.
- **Trigger:** the element itself (`asChild`), which must be focusable: a button, a link,
  or an element with `tabIndex={0}`.

## States

- **Closed, open.** A tooltip opens after a short delay on hover and at once on keyboard
  focus. It stays open while the pointer is over it, so a person can read it. Escape
  closes it.
- **Why a control is unavailable.** A native `disabled` button receives no pointer events
  and no focus, so its tooltip can never open. Keep it focusable instead:
  `Button` with `disabled` and `focusableWhenDisabled`. It stays in the tab order, swallows
  clicks and Enter, and the tooltip explains.

## Accessibility

- A tooltip is linked to its trigger with `aria-describedby`; it is a description, not a
  name. An icon-only button still needs its own `aria-label`; the tooltip repeats it for
  sighted pointer users.
- Open on focus as well as hover, close on Escape without moving focus, and do not hide
  while the pointer moves from the trigger onto the tooltip (WCAG 1.4.13: dismissible,
  hoverable, persistent).
- Do not put interactive content inside. A tooltip is not a popover.
- Keep the text short and plain. It is read to screen reader users after the trigger's
  name.
- A hover card is announced when it opens only as far as its content is reachable; do not
  rely on it for anything the person must know.

## CSP notes

Neither creates a `style` element. Their position comes from the floating layer as
CSSOM style writes, which `style-src 'self'` allows.

## Copy

- A tooltip is a noun phrase or a short sentence without a period: "Copy plan hash",
  "Apply needs an approval from another person".
- A reason says what is missing and who can fix it, not that the control is disabled.
- Do not repeat the visible label of the trigger.
- A hover card starts with the name of the thing in medium weight, then one fact per line,
  with the source and age of a read last (see
  [Truthful states](/guidance/truthful-states)).
