# Card

`Card` is a bordered, slightly raised container that groups the facts or the fields of
one thing: a cluster, a policy, a setting. Its parts are `Card`, `CardHeader`,
`CardTitle`, `CardDescription`, `CardContent` and `CardFooter`. A card is a layout
component; it has no behavior, no link and no click of its own.

## When to use

- Use a card to give one thing a visual boundary on a page of several things: the
  summaries on an overview, the fields of one setting on a settings page.
- Do not wrap a whole page in a card, nest cards, or make a card the only target of a
  click. If a card should lead somewhere, put a link or a button inside it. If the
  content is a long list of rows of the same kind, use a [table](/ui/table).
- Do not use a card to decorate. Space and a hairline separate content as well.

## Anatomy

- **Card:** `rounded-lg`, a border, the `surface-100` background and a faint shadow. It clips its
  contents (`overflow-hidden`) so the corners stay round.
- **Header:** padded, bordered below. **Title** (`CardTitle`, an `h3`): small, monospace,
  uppercase. **Description**: one sentence, quiet.
- **Content:** one or more sections, each padded and separated from the next by a border;
  the last has none.
- **Footer:** padded, in a row, for the actions of the card (save, open).
- The inner horizontal padding is the `--card-padding-x` variable (16 px; 24 px for the
  larger step), so the header, the sections and the footer line up.

## States

A card has none. Its contents have the states: a card whose read failed shows the failed
state inside it; a card whose value is stale shows the banner and the age.

## Accessibility

- `CardTitle` is always an `h3`, so place cards under a section heading (`h2`) to keep the
  outline of the page correct. A card on its own is not a region and needs no role.
- If a card is a distinct region of the page that people jump to, give it an `aria-label`
  or `aria-labelledby` and `role="region"`; do not do it for every card.
- Do not rely on the border to separate cards for people who cannot see it: the headings
  and the order also separate them.
- Interactive content inside keeps its own labels and focus rings.

## Copy

- The title is a short noun phrase ("Approval window"), shown in uppercase by the style
  but written in sentence case.
- The description states the effect or the constraint in the present tense.
- The footer button names the outcome ("Save policy"), and a footer hint states what
  happens next.
