# Block F: readiness scorecard for Wave 11

The question this campaign answers: **can REOSA's production database be managed by this system
(Wave 11) without surprises, and what stops that today?** This is evidence for the owner; it does not
authorize Wave 11. Commit under test `d51da5d0`. `VC-nnn` are the finding ids of
`../2026-10-28-verification-fixes.md` (section 4); `R/`, `A/`, `B/`, `C/`, `D/`, `E/` are folders of this evidence folder.

Verdicts: **PASS** (proven on the disposable stack or the pinned suites), **FAIL** (a reproduced finding blocks it),
**UNPROVEN** (not run, or only read from code).

## What must be true before the owner points this at production

1. **The two outage-class items are closed or accepted:** the Control Store disk (VC-001: a holder of `migrations.plan` can fill it
   and the rows cannot be deleted) and the memory exhaustion by an allowlisted read (VC-002). The owner decides whether they count as P0.
2. **A restore proof cannot pass falsely:** VC-004 and VC-005 fixed (replay to the archive end, WAL continuity in `backup.verify`, a
   mandatory data check in a drill), then one real drill with a measured RTO **and** an RPO on `reos-db-01`'s own layout with the
   node agent and an adopted pgBackRest repository (the storage part of OD-4 is open).
3. **The installation cannot be wedged by one operation:** VC-006, VC-007, VC-008 (a human close that never re-executes, a reconcile of
   unknown outcomes, no head-of-line blocking), plus VC-003 (a clock step must not brick the audit chain).
4. **The upgrade and recovery runbooks match the code:** VC-009, VC-010, VC-011 fixed or rewritten, and one rehearsal on the owner's own
   deployment layout (Coolify containers with the real limits) following the written card. Until then **do not redeploy ExponetDB**.
5. **The planner tells the truth about the schema it will change:** VC-016 and VC-017 (`serial`), the catalog facts for lock and rewrite
   classes, and a per-finding acknowledgement for false positives (owner decision). Until then avoid `serial`/`bigserial` in bundles
   (identity columns apply) and read the lock text as a lower bound.
6. **The evidence rule means what it says:** VC-079 (mirror cross-check and operation attribution) and the approval deadline (VC-057).
7. **Hostile or merely large input cannot take serve down:** VC-013, VC-014, VC-015, VC-046, VC-068 with named limits.
8. **Process:** branch protection with the 18 checks required (VC-018), the three missing checks in CI, a stable `node-pg` job, and the first
   campaign's still-open findings (28 NOT FIXED) triaged: `R/verdicts.md`.

What the evidence says is already good: the single-writer lock, atomicity of a bundle, the order of applied and verified, the audit chain,
no secret leak in 15 sinks, host containment, a fast and exact restore engine, an upgrade that loses nothing going forward, and a dress
rehearsal on a 4.4 GB Supabase-like schema (306 tables, 150 hostile history files) that ran import, baseline, plan, apply under load,
drift, PITR, reconcile, promotion and replay end to end with every difference explained.
