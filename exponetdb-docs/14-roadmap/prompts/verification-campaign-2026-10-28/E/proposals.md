## P-01 Recovery command for `needs_attention`, and fair claiming `[OPEN]`

**Why (re-judged).** S1 reproduced the dead end on a stack for `migration.rollback`, `ledger.reconcile`, `drift.accept` and for apply outcome `unknown`: `retryable=false`, `OPERATION_NOT_RETRYABLE`, `OPERATION_NOT_CANCELABLE`, `operation_locks` still holds `target:<id>:schema`, and `operation.force_release` is in none of the four operation routes (VC-006, VC-007, VC-056). The same parked operation starves the installation: P2 on another target stayed `queued` 73 s while the blocked one wrote 70 events in 72 s, and ran at once when the blocker was cancelled (VC-008). The one existing exit closes before it checks (VC-045, reproduced: the stuck operation ends `verification_failed`, its lock released, though the recovery was refused), is class `low` on production, and mislabels (VC-051). A committed change with a lost COMMIT reply parks as `unknown`, is never reconciled once the target returns, and its only exit calls a fully applied migration `partially_applied` (VC-059). `needs_attention` is routine, not rare: a missing verifier credential makes it the normal end of an apply (VC-055, P-09).

**Design sketch.** (1) `operation.close`: human only, class `high`, re-authentication, reason required, names the operation and the domains it frees, shows the live ledger read; never re-executes (C-16); the closer states the observed outcome; the engine does not rewrite `unknown`. Show `closedBy` in every view (D-E1-6 option a). (2) Order fix in `driver.ts:178-186`: bindings and admission before any close (VC-045). (3) `blocked_until` or `waiting_for` on the operation row so a blocked operation is not re-claimed every tick, and `locks_waiting` is written only when the blocker changes (VC-008). (4) A timer-driven reconcile for `unknown` once the target answers (VC-059). (5) Then per-kind verify retry or `supersedes` for node, backup, registry and drift kinds; the CLI hint uses the server's `retryable` flag (VC-050). (6) Decide D0-01 with it: a verify plan that names a stuck operation on production needs approval and re-authentication.

**Cost.** M (S for 1 and 2; the claim change is small but touches the hot loop). **Risk.** Closing an operation whose change is half-applied: mitigated by the live ledger read and the stated outcome; a close that becomes a habit: keep it `high`, human and counted in the audit summary. **Recommendation: do first.** The acceptance test is `I09-exits` plus S1's `I9-exits.sh` scenarios; the claim fix has its own two-domain test (VC-008).

## P-02 Control Plane foundations: Control Store volume, audit-chain clock guard, keyring custody `[OPEN]` (new)

**Why.** Three P1 findings show that the Control Plane depends on a disk, a clock and a key file that it does not meter, check or repair. **Disk (VC-001):** five ingests with a `migrations.plan` token took free space from 296 MB to 53 MB with no audit event; the next write was a PostgreSQL PANIC, the container exited and a plain restart failed; rows cannot be deleted (append-only); growing the volume recovered it in 3.8 s. **Clock (VC-003):** a +3 h step and back left the audit chain refusing every event for 2 h 54 min (`audit.chain_guard`, `occurred_at < head_at`), login, plan and apply answering 500, readiness `ready`, reads working; the store had to be rebuilt to continue (also VC-041, VC-043, VC-151). **Keyring (VC-011):** with a new keyring, roles stay `not_ready`, plan and login answer 500, nothing marks secrets lost; RB-14 cannot start; the offline copy is the whole recovery. Around these: store errors are 500 `INTERNAL` (VC-037), roles stay `not_ready` after the cause clears (VC-078), and `default_transaction_read_only` does not stop writes because every write transaction opens `READ WRITE` (`store.ts:158`; S6).

**Design sketch.** (a) A Control Store **volume component** in installation health (free bytes and growth; warn at 80 %, refuse ingest at 90 %), audit every ingest in its transaction, a per-identity daily byte quota, an audited human-only purge of never-planned revisions (the 90-day retention class of `data-retention.md`, which has no pruning code: VC-042), a separate WAL volume and restart policy in the deployment record, a runbook "Control Store will not start: ENOSPC". (b) **Audit chain:** a readiness check on audit append, a typed `AUDIT_CLOCK_SKEW`, a skew guard (refuse or clamp future events with a marker, ideally against a monotonic source), a documented, audited repair operation for a head ahead of real time, and a skew check between serve, worker, gateway and the database (VC-041). (c) **Keyring:** custody in RB-14 (second offline copy, a yearly restore test) or an audited reset operation that marks lost secrets gone and lets the owner submit new ones; this is an owner decision because it touches C-20. (d) Self-observation that the roles can already compute: the `/unmatched` route label, an audit-gate probe in readiness, typed Control Store errors (VC-036 to VC-038).

**Cost.** (a) S to M, (b) M, (c) S as words and M as an operation, (d) S. **Risk.** A purge path next to append-only tables must be limited to rows no plan or ledger row references; an audit-chain repair operation touches the integrity primitive (C-31) and needs an ADR. **Recommendation: do first** for (a), the readiness check and typed error of (b), the words of (c) and (d); decide the repair operation and the reset with the owner. These are Group A of the fix order.

## P-03 Plan-time risk facts, a hold-time estimate and a machine-readable risk report `[OPEN]`

**Why (re-judged with S5).** The brief's preflight estimates lock risk from `pg_stat` and table sizes. The evidence now has a measurement: on a 4.4 GB database at 300 tps, an index build on 5M rows and a type change on 1.5M rows took 16.6 s; p99 went from 6 ms to **8.75 s during the window**, 48 % of the transactions in it took over 1 s, no query failed (`B/rehearsal/README.md`). The plan said lock severity `high/high/high/critical/high`, `destructive`, allowed: severity labels carry no duration. What predicts the window is **relation size times rewrite class**, and the planner already reads sizes. What the activity read can add is smaller than it looks: with the default 5 s `lock_timeout`, a DDL queued behind a 9 s reader gave up after 5 s and a SELECT behind it waited 4.1 s (`B/ddl-hazard/evidence/pgcheck-02-multi-session.txt`); it saves a failed attempt, it does not prevent harm, and it is stale by apply time. The harm in B3 comes from misclassification: `ADD COLUMN bigserial` is "brief" and rewrites under ACCESS EXCLUSIVE (VC-017), `DEFAULT now()` and `varchar(200)` to `varchar(500)` are blocked though they do not rewrite (VC-029, VC-030), `SET NOT NULL` after a validated CHECK and `UNIQUE USING INDEX` are blocked though they scan nothing (VC-031, VC-032), a cheap lock held across a later slow statement is "brief" (VC-033), `serial` in `CREATE TABLE` cannot be applied at all (VC-016), and an `ALTER COLUMN TYPE` on a column a view reads fails `0A000` after earlier bundles applied (VC-071). A false positive has no lint-clean form, so people lower the rule for the whole project (`F/decisions-B3.md`).

**Design sketch.** (a) Catalog facts in the planner: column types and typmods, `provolatile`, validated `CHECK (col IS NOT NULL)` into `provenNotNull`, `indexname` of `USING INDEX`, dependent objects from `pg_depend` (views, materialized views, policies, defaults, indexes), `indisvalid`; parser fixes for serial, identity and `concurrent` on DETACH (VC-034); lock lifetime to COMMIT; `long_lock_timeout` blocking above 60 s (VC-035). (b) A **hold-time estimate** per statement: size divided by a throughput constant per rewrite class, shown as a range with its `basis`, calibrated by the durations of earlier applies on the same target (the per-migration progress rows hold them). (c) Later: an advisory activity read (oldest open transactions on the affected relations, with source and time) and an opt-in bounded `lockRetry` for transactional bundles (a decision against the spirit of C-12). (d) Later: a closed schema `PlanRiskReport@1` with `holdUntil`, `rewrite`, `scans`, `basis` (`parser`, `catalog`, `measured`, `unknown`), sizes with time, lowered rules, and author-derived strings in `untrusted` members (VC-107), served by `plan_get`, REST, `--output json` and a `migration plan --check` exit code. (e) A per-finding acknowledgement only for what facts cannot decide; it needs the store to keep metadata edits first (VC-083).

**Cost.** (a) M, (b) S to M, (c) S, (d) S to M. **Risk.** More reads from the target add trust-boundary rows (`E/structure.md` §9): estimates only, never authorization; a classifier that knows more can be wrong with more confidence, so `unknown` stays explicit (C-30); publishing the report before (a) pins today's misclassifications (C-33). PostgreSQL 16 and 18 and the "under load" cells of the hazard matrix are still unmeasured. **Recommendation: do first** for (a) and (b); **later** for (c), (d), (e).

## P-05 One work budget per request, caps, target-side read bounds, an analysis lane `[OPEN]`

**Why.** Every bound is per parse, per file or per statement (`E/structure.md` §10). Serve side: VC-013 (38 s), VC-014 (exit 134), VC-015 (about 20 min), VC-026 (61 s at 800), VC-047 (32 MiB parsed before authorization), VC-069 (S4: under 1 MB costs 31 s or 620 MiB), VC-079 (S6: 1,000 concurrent MCP requests take `serve` from 156 to 583 MB before the limiter answers 429). Target side: VC-002 (an allowlisted `array_agg(repeat(...))` restarts the cluster, about 4 s, a bystander dropped), VC-012 (a read whose backend dies never returns; two wedge the slots), VC-048 (temp files). **Design sketch.** (1) Caps (S): nesting above 32 refused with a named reason; comment openers counted in the token bound; the linear regex; `admit` before the body of a route over the default limit; at most limit + 1 bytes read in the CLI; a named `parse_budget_exceeded`. (3) Target side (S to M): size-argument rules for `repeat`, `lpad`, `rpad`, `array_fill` and unbounded `generate_series`; a hard deadline for reads that never awaits `close()` on a dead client; a memory fact in the registry. (4) The lane (M to L): `serve` admits and seals, the worker analyses in a worker thread with a deadline, the Control Store transaction no longer spans analysis or live reads (VC-122).

**Cost.** S, M, S to M, M to L. **Risk.** The lane adds a "no live worker" failure mode and latency; caps change behavior for large legitimate files (the token bound already makes 100,000 tiny statements impossible: VC-094), so publish them. **Recommendation: do first** for (1) to (3) (Groups A and E); **later** for (4), only if the caps leave a P1-scale freeze. The owner decides whether VC-001 and VC-002 count as P0 (fix prompt, owner decisions).

## P-06 The upgrade card, the rollback truth and a compatibility table `[OPEN]`

**Why.** S3 measured it: the documented backup is a `pg_dumpall` of 556 ms; the upgrade takes 18.3 to 19.0 s and all three roles are not ready from the moment `migrate` commits; the documented rollback exits 3 `schema_newer` in 0.3 s (VC-009); the real one, restoring the dump, takes 7.0 s but loses everything written after it (48 audit events, 6 operations, 6 plans here), blocks every touched target with `LEDGER_DIVERGENCE` and costs 6 steps per nonprod target and 10 on production (VC-010); RB-10 step 6 is empty for managed targets (VC-053); the runbook omits 11 steps (VC-066: no backup, no outage window, no drain, no preflight, no post-checks); a redeploy with Docker's default stop time kills a running apply (VC-044); an old role keeps claiming after the schema moved ahead (VC-065); a restored production target the store never mirrored is not blocked and its unknown row is adopted by the next apply (VC-064); the second RB-10 recovery fails (VC-140). An old plan applied normally across the upgrade, so the 500 of VC-054 needs a kind-version bump to appear.

**Design sketch.** (1) The **card**, one page: dump, `gzip -t`, scratch restore, `control-store migrate --plan`, expected outage (about 16 s of no Web App), deploy order, five post-checks (`control-store status` `current`, `keyring status`, `audit verify`, node `active`, one plan and apply), and "if it goes wrong **do not start the old image, restore the dump**" with the consequences and the per-target reconcile. (2) `schema_newer` prints expected, applied and "restore the pre-upgrade dump (RB-10)"; `LEDGER_DIVERGENCE` names `migration ledger reconcile --target <path> --note ...`. (3) A numbered mandatory verified dump; `control-store migrate --plan` (lists the bundles, flags additive ones); a worker drain (stop claiming, wait until none runs) and a stop grace period above the 25 s timeout. (4) A run-time schema check so an old role stops claiming. (5) The compatibility table: engine release by PostgreSQL major (16, 17, 18), manifest and c1, node protocol, Control Store schema ids, each cell naming the CI job that proves it; the three libpg_query builds and the 17/18-only behaviors (U+000B, `transaction_timeout`, `NOT NULL ... NOT VALID`) are the first rows. (6) Not now: an additive-compatible start (an ADR; the old binary would misread a schema if a bundle were mislabeled). With a 7 s restore and one installation, downtime is acceptable; the cost of a rollback is the lost window and the reconcile, which (1) states.

**Cost.** (1), (2), (5) S; (3) M; (4) S. **Risk.** Low. **Recommendation: do first** (Group D); gate on the compatibility table later.

## P-07 A backup proof that compares data `[OPEN]` (new)

**Why.** S2 at 7.6 GB measured the numbers the brief asked for: full backup 19.2 s, restore to the end of the archive 67.3 s, PITR 39.8 to 57.3 s with exact row counts, drills RTO 35 to 131 s (real), drill operation wall 2.1 times the RTO. The same run shows the proof can lie (VC-004): with the newest WAL segment deleted, `backup.verify` `succeeded`, the restore `succeeded` and the drill `passed` with RTO 35 s while **12 committed rows were missing**; corrupted, 4 rows; a middle segment deleted, verify `succeeded` (VC-005); the stop segment of the newest full deleted, verify `succeeded` and the backup unrestorable. The root cause is that the replay target is the start of the newest segment (`selection.ts:98`) and `wal_coverage.gaps` is never written. The drill record carries no recovery point (VC-136). Also restic restores stop without the roles (VC-060), `restore.execute` is accepted though `prepare` said `space_insufficient` (VC-061), a wrong key takes 102 s and ends `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` (VC-062), `pitr_random` is refused until an observation exists (VC-063).

**Design sketch.** (1) Continuity computed from the archive's segment list: write `wal_coverage.gaps`; `backup.verify` fails on a gap inside any backup's start..stop and between the newest backup and the archive end; `restore.prepare` sets `walContinuous`. (2) The drill target is the end of the last archived segment (`walTo = segmentLsn(next)`), with an explicit exception for the archive's current partial segment. (3) The drill compares a **recovery point**: it records the last replayed LSN and time and compares it with the source's last archived LSN, and reports the observed RPO; a marker row option for owners who want a data check (OD-60). (4) `restore.execute` re-checks space on the scratch directory; the restic runbook stops without globals; helper refusals surface with their code.

**Cost.** M. **Risk.** False failures at the archive's normal partial segment; mitigated by the exception. **Recommendation: do first.** "A restore is proven with a measured number" is a Wave 11 precondition; today the number is real but the pass is not (Group C).

## P-08 Ledger watch, generation guard and identity in health `[OPEN]`

**Why.** `ledger.md:236` documents a 10-minute reconciliation and a finding; none exists (VC-023). S5 shows what the operator sees after a restore: `target show` healthy, `Ledger: unknown`, `Mirror: out of sync`, `LEDGER_DIVERGENCE` only at the first plan, "no finding, no health condition". S3 shows it worse: a never-mirrored production target is not blocked and the next apply adopts its unknown row without a decision (VC-064). `generation` never changes (VC-024). The evidence rule trusts an operation id (VC-020, static; the forged-evidence run is **pending S7**). A clone behind the registered name, a hot standby and a foreign instance read healthy or suspend targets within seconds (VC-072, VC-074, VC-075), and a renamed database is `INTERNAL` (VC-073).

**Design sketch.** (1) A worker task `ledger.compare` per active target (observer read, every mirrored column including `reverted_at` and `fingerprint_after`), a critical finding `ledger_divergence`, an audit event and a metric; skipped while an operation holds the schema domain. (2) "A mirror for an earlier generation and none for the current one" and "ledger rows no operation of this target wrote" are divergences; `ledger reconcile` must accept the never-mirrored case. (3) Evidence only from the mirror and the Control Store's verification record (VC-020). (4) Health reports database oid, system identifier and recovery state, so a clone or standby is named, not healthy.

**Cost.** (1) and (2) S, (3) S, (4) S to M. **Risk.** One connection per target every 10 minutes; false findings in the crash-to-mirror window if the skip rule is wrong. **Recommendation: do first** (Group G).

## P-09 Apply-readiness check per target `[OPEN]` (new)

**Why.** Preconditions of a safe production apply are known but surface late: the verifier credential (VC-055: the apply commits, verification ends `error`, the target is parked); a verified backup newer than 24 h for destructive plans, which S5 could not satisfy and whose absence answers `500 INTERNAL` with no operation and no log line (VC-067; S5-D6); node and certificate state (a target certificate with 5 days left is `healthy` with no finding: VC-040); clock skew (VC-041); an open drift baseline behind a part-way plan (VC-070); no break-glass channel (VC-117).

**Design sketch.** (1) At plan time, a `verifierReady` fact from the registry's credentials and `verifierProblems`; a production plan is denied when false. (2) Later `exponetdb target check <target> --for apply`: pass / fail / unknown per check, each with source, time and the fixing command; `unknown` is never green (C-30); it reports and the policy engine decides. (3) The unmet-backup refusal becomes a typed `BACKUP_REQUIRED`.

**Cost.** (1) S, (2) S to M. **Risk.** A stale green; mitigated by timestamps. **Recommendation: do first** for (1) and (3); **later** for (2).

## P-12 Rehearsal apply on a throwaway clone `[OPEN]`

**Why for (re-judged).** S5 is itself a rehearsal and shows the class it catches: the plan was silent on a view dependency, and the apply failed `0A000` on the fourth bundle after three had applied (VC-071); on an empty-database replay, S5 explained every difference between replay and live (+7 -7 ~6 of 4,260 objects, 8.9 s). The clone is cheap at S2's size: restore to the end of the archive took 67 s at 7.6 GB and a drill's operation 99 to 220 s. **Why not yet.** The source is only as good as the proof (P-07: VC-004, VC-005); `restore.execute` ignores `space_insufficient` (VC-061); a restore cannot be registered as a target (no placement move; VC-076); and a clone without traffic measures duration (16.6 s in S5), not the 8.75 s p99 the traffic saw, and not lock queueing behind live readers. A copy of production data into a scratch cluster needs an access and retention rule (C-1). REOSA's own table sizes are not in the evidence.

**Design sketch.** A `migration.rehearse` kind: restore the latest verified backup into a drill scratch cluster, register it as an ephemeral target in a `rehearsal` environment, apply the production plan's ids and checksums, record durations, relfilenode changes, lock hold and verification, attach the result to the production plan as advisory evidence, discard. **Cost.** L. **Risk.** Time and disk; a false sense of safety; data governance. **Recommendation: later, but right after P-07 and P-03.** Practice now: refresh staging from the latest verified production backup before a release, so the staging-evidence rule is a rehearsal on production-sized data, and use S5's scripts (`B/rehearsal/scripts/`) as the checklist.

## P-13 Promotion command, release run and release sets `[OPEN]`

**Why.** There is no promotion object (DF-24); the default CLI plan cannot express "promote what is verified on staging" beyond 50 bundles (VC-022). S5 shows the gates work (a never-verified checksum and an altered bundle are refused) and shows the structure problem: one hazardous bundle blocks every later one of the chain at production (S5-D1). A plan that stops part-way leaves the drift baseline behind: 34 findings, 6 critical, until a person accepts all (VC-070). The hotfix follow-up is unbuilt (VC-086). A non-transactional bundle runs alone, so a release is several plans with nothing that holds them together.

**Judgement.** A named list of (id, checksum, digest) adds mostly a name; the plan already binds list, order, evidence and approval by hash. The real gaps: (1) `migration promote --from <env> --to <env>`, planning exactly the ids verified on the evidence environment and pending on the target (also removes the 50 limit); (2) an ordered **release run** that applies several plans in sequence and halts on the first failure, with the drift baseline advanced per migration; (3) a promotion record as a read model joining plan, evidence, operation and verification ids. **Cost.** S for (1) (rides with P-04), M for (2) and (3). **Risk.** A new object duplicating plan semantics; each plan still needs its own approval. **Recommendation: later.**

## P-14 A batched data-migration operation kind `[OPEN]` (new)

**Why.** A transactional bundle is one transaction and `COMMIT` is forbidden, so the safe form of an `UPDATE` backfill, and of the documented NOT NULL recipe on a large table, cannot be a bundle (`engine_expressible=no`, B3-D1); `data_change_unbounded` fires only for a missing `WHERE`. S5's tables (5M rows) show what an unbatched type change or index costs in window length; no unbatched backfill was measured.

**Design sketch.** A kind `data.backfill`: predicate, key, batch size and maximum rate in the plan; resumable by watermark; throttled on lock waits and replication lag; one operation with progress events; verification by count or checksum; a ledger row per backfill. The hard part is C-6, C-12, C-16 and C-18 with intermediate commits. **Cost.** L. **Risk.** A new write path (half-done at a watermark, lag, autovacuum). **Recommendation: later; decide at REOSA's first real backfill.** Until then document an out-of-band procedure with an audit note.

## P-15 Bundle signing and provenance `[OPEN]`

**Why the question comes up.** The evidence rule trusts operation ids on rows a superuser can write (VC-020), and `migration.resume` takes the operation to close from target events (VC-105). **What the evidence says.** These are holes of attribution, not of authenticity. S4 confirms the gates hold on the cases run: evidence from the wrong environment is `deny`, altered bytes under an applied id are `MIGRATION_CHECKSUM_MISMATCH`, an agent's production plan waits for a person, and S5's cheats A and B were refused. A signature proves "these bytes came from a reviewed commit"; it adds assurance only if a policy demands it, and `migration.agent-production` already requires a human. An HMAC on ledger rows would defend against a hand-written row, but the mirror cross-check of P-08 gives the same protection with no key custody, and neither helps against a restore replaying valid old rows. **Cost.** M to L (key custody under C-20, rotation, an envelope outside c1). **Recommendation: not needed now.** Instead: P-08(3); store the manifest's `source` claim (commit, CI run, author) as attributed, untrusted metadata per revision and show it to the approver (needs VC-083 first); revisit with a CI OIDC attestation if agents gain inline-bundle rights on production or more than one team authors. **pending S7:** the forged-evidence run behind VC-020 does not change this judgement, only whether P-08(3) is a bug fix or a demonstrated hole.
